Google Search Console shows how your site does **in Google search, before the click**: which searches showed your pages, how often people clicked, and your position. Google Analytics 4 shows what happens **on your site, after the click**, from every source: how many people came, from where, which pages they read and whether they signed up.

They're separate free tools from Google that answer different questions, and they work best together.

## The difference at a glance

|                           | Search Console                                      | Google Analytics 4                                         |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Answers                   | How do people find me on Google search?             | What do people do once they're on my site?                 |
| Covers                    | Google search only                                  | Every source: Google, social, email, AI assistants, direct |
| Main numbers              | Clicks, impressions, click rate, average position   | Visitors (active users), sessions, views, key events       |
| Shows the searches?       | Yes, the words people typed                         | No: Google hides them, so organic search shows as "google" |
| Needs a tag on your site? | No: you verify the site once                        | Yes: the GA4 tag on every page                             |
| Counts                    | Clicks from Google's results                        | People whose browser ran the GA4 tag (and allowed cookies) |
| Also checks               | Indexing, sitemaps, page experience, manual actions | Realtime, landing pages, countries, devices, events        |

## Is Search Console part of Google Analytics?

No. They're two products with their own sign-ins and reports. You can **link** them, so GA4 shows Search Console's numbers next to its own (see below), but each keeps working on its own.

## Why the numbers don't match

Search Console's **clicks** and GA4's **Google organic visitors** never match exactly:

- Search Console counts clicks; GA4 counts people whose browser ran the tag. Ad blockers, a declined cookie banner or someone leaving before the page loads mean a click with no GA4 visit.
- One person can click several times (several clicks, one visitor).
- Search Console uses Pacific Time days; GA4 uses your property's time zone.

So a gap is normal; watch whether both move the same way. [Why tools show different numbers](/guides/ga4-numbers-differ).

## Which one should I use?

Both, for different questions:

- "Which searches bring people, and which pages could rank higher?" → Search Console. [How to use it](/guides/google-search-console).
- "How many people came, from where, and did they sign up?" → Google Analytics. [How to read it](/guides/understand-website-traffic).

## How to see them together

Link them, then GA4 gets two extra reports: **Queries** (the searches) and **Google organic search traffic** (landing pages with both tools' numbers).

<Steps>
<Step>

Make sure the site is in Search Console ([how](/guides/add-site-to-search-console)) and that you have the Editor role in GA4 and are a verified owner in Search Console.

</Step>
<Step>

In GA4, open **Admin**, and under **Product links** click **Search Console links**, then **Link**.

</Step>
<Step>

Choose the Search Console property, then the GA4 web data stream for the same site, and submit.

</Step>
<Step>

The Search Console reports are unpublished at first: open **Reports** → **Library**, find the **Search Console** collection and publish it.

</Step>
</Steps>

<InSimpleDash title="Both on one page">
  SimpleDash reads both: visitors, sources and signups from GA4, and with Pro, Google search
  insights from Search Console (clicks, how often you were seen, the searches, and one page to work
  on each week), in plain words on the same dashboard. [How it works](/docs/google-search).
</InSimpleDash>
