Google Analytics 4 (GA4) is Google's free tool that counts the people who visit your website or app: how many came, where they came from, which pages they read and whether they did what you wanted, like signing up. It's the current version of Google Analytics, so today "Google Analytics" and "GA4" mean the same thing.

It works through a small tag (a piece of code) on every page that reports each visit to Google.

## Is Google Analytics free?

Yes. GA4 is free for websites and apps of any size, with no time limit. Large companies can pay for **Google Analytics 360**, which raises limits and adds support, but small sites never need it.

What can cost you something: time to set it up and learn it, and, in places like the EU and UK, a cookie banner ([do you need one?](/guides/ga4-cookie-consent)).

## What can Google Analytics tell you?

| Question                     | Where in GA4                           |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| How many people came?        | Acquisition overview: active users     |
| Where did they come from?    | Traffic acquisition: source or channel |
| Which pages do they read?    | Pages and screens                      |
| Where do visits start?       | Landing page                           |
| Did they sign up or buy?     | Key events                             |
| Who's on the site right now? | Realtime                               |
| Which countries and devices? | User attributes, and Tech              |

[How to read these](/guides/understand-website-traffic).

## What can't it tell you?

- **Who** a visitor is. GA4 doesn't show names or emails, and Google's rules forbid sending them.
- **What people searched on Google.** That's in Search Console ([the difference](/guides/google-analytics-vs-search-console)).
- **Anything before you added the tag.** GA4 starts counting the day it's installed.
- **Other people's websites.** You only see sites you have access to.
- **Everyone.** Ad blockers and declined cookie banners mean some visits aren't counted.

## Is GA4 the same as Google Analytics?

Yes, today. The old version, Universal Analytics, stopped collecting data on July 1, 2023, and GA4 replaced it. GA4 counts **events** (every page view, scroll and click) instead of page views and sessions first, and renamed some things: goals and conversions became [key events](/guides/ga4-key-events), and "users" usually means [active users](/glossary/active-users).

## Is GA4 hard to learn?

The basics aren't: about five numbers answer most questions for a small site. The rest of GA4 is built for analysts and advertisers, which is why it feels hard. [Why GA4 feels complicated, and what to look at](/guides/ga4-too-complicated).
