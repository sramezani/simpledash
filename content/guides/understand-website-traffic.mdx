For a small site, Google Analytics comes down to three questions: how many people came, where they came from, and whether they did what you wanted (sign up or buy). Answer those every week, compared with the week before, and you can ignore most of GA4's other reports.

## Question 1: how many came?

**Visitors** (GA4's [active users](/glossary/active-users)) is the number that matters. Page views and [sessions](/glossary/sessions) are useful context, but they grow when the same people browse more.

Where in GA4: Reports → **Acquisition overview**, the Active users card.

## Question 2: where did they come from?

**Sources and channels**: Google, X, a newsletter, an AI assistant, or Direct. A few sources usually bring most visitors; watch those.

Where in GA4: Reports → Acquisition → **Traffic acquisition**, by Session source or Session default channel group.

Things to know:

- **Direct** is often apps and emails that hide where a click came from. [Why it's high](/guides/ga4-direct-traffic).
- **AI assistants** are growing. [See them](/guides/ga4-ai-traffic).
- **Tag what you share** with [UTM links](/guides/utm-links), so launches and newsletters show up by name.

## Question 3: did they do what you wanted?

**Signups and sales**, as [key events](/guides/ga4-key-events). Without them, you only know how many came, not whether it worked.

Where in GA4: Reports → Engagement → **Events**, or the Key events column in Traffic acquisition.

## The pages that bring people in

**Landing pages** show where visits start: your homepage, a blog post, a docs page. A post that keeps bringing visitors is worth updating and linking to.

Where in GA4: Reports → Engagement → **Landing page**.

## What to ignore early on

- **Bounce rate** on its own. GA4 calculates it differently from the old Universal Analytics. [Engaged sessions](/glossary/engaged-sessions) explains it.
- **Daily ups and downs.** Compare weeks, not days.
- **Small numbers.** Ten visitors one week and fifteen the next isn't a trend.

## A weekly habit

Once a week, look at visitors, top sources and signups against the week before, and write one line about what changed. That's more useful than an hour in GA4 once a month.
