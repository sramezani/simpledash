When traffic drops, first check it's real: tracking breaks more often than traffic does. Then narrow it down: which source fell, which pages, which devices or countries, and, for Google, which searches. That tells you _what_ changed; the _why_ usually becomes obvious from there.

## 1. Rule out a tracking problem

- **Is GA4 still running?** Open your site and check GA4's Realtime report. [How](/guides/check-ga4-is-working).
- **Did anything change on your site?** A redesign, a new cookie banner, a new theme or a removed plugin can drop the tag.
- **Is the day complete?** Today and yesterday are still filling up. [Why GA4 is late](/guides/ga4-data-delay).

If every source fell at once, on one day, to near zero, it's almost always tracking.

## 2. Compare the right periods

Compare the same length of time, and the same days of the week. A Monday-to-Sunday week against the week before is fair; a weekend against weekdays isn't. For seasonal sites, also compare with the same period last year.

## 3. Find which source fell

In GA4: Reports → Acquisition → **Traffic acquisition**, with **Compare** on (the date picker → Compare). Sort by the change. Usually one or two sources explain most of the drop.

| The drop is mostly in   | Look at                                                                     |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Organic search (Google) | Search Console: which pages and searches lost clicks (step 5)               |
| Referral                | Did a site that linked to you remove the link, or did a post stop trending? |
| Social                  | Did a post bring a spike last period?                                       |
| Direct                  | Did a newsletter or chat share happen last period?                          |
| Everything at once      | Tracking (step 1), or the site was down                                     |

## 4. Find which pages fell

Reports → Engagement → **Landing page**, compared with the period before. A single page losing most of its visits points to a ranking change or a lost link for that page.

## 5. Check Search Console for Google drops

In Search Console → Performance → Search results, compare the two periods and sort pages by the change in clicks. For each page, check whether **position** fell (a ranking change) or **impressions** fell (fewer searches, or seasonal). [Find which searches changed](/guides/google-clicks-what-changed).

## 6. Write down what you find

Add a note on the day it started, so next time you remember what happened.
