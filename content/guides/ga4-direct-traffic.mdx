"Direct" in Google Analytics 4 means GA4 couldn't tell where a visit came from. Some of it is real (people typing your address or using a bookmark), but a lot is visits from apps, emails and documents that don't pass on where the click happened. You can't make it zero, but tagged links and a few settings bring it down.

## What GA4 counts as direct

A visit is **Direct** (source `(direct)`, medium `(none)`) when the browser sends no referrer and the link has no UTM tags. That happens when someone:

- types your address or uses a bookmark or the browser's autocomplete
- clicks a link in an app that hides the referrer: WhatsApp, Slack, Discord, Telegram, many email apps, some AI assistant apps
- clicks a link in a PDF, a Word document or a desktop app
- follows a redirect that drops the referrer
- comes from an `https` page to an `http` page

## Common reasons it's high on small sites

1. **Your own visits.** On a small site, you and your team can be a big share. [Exclude internal traffic](/guides/exclude-internal-traffic-ga4).
2. **Newsletters and messages.** Links in emails and chats often arrive without a referrer. Tag them with [UTM links](/guides/utm-links).
3. **Shared links.** When your link spreads in group chats, it shows as direct.
4. **New tracking.** In the first days after adding GA4, returning visitors with bookmarks make direct look big.

## What you can fix

| Cause                                     | Fix                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Your own visits                           | [Define internal traffic and turn the filter on](/guides/exclude-internal-traffic-ga4) |
| Emails, newsletters, ads, links you share | Add [UTM tags](/guides/utm-links)                                                      |
| Payment pages taking over visits          | [List unwanted referrals](/guides/ga4-unwanted-referrals-stripe)                       |
| Redirects dropping the referrer           | Link straight to the final address                                                     |

What you can't fix: people who type your address, and apps that hide where a click came from. That part of direct is normal, and often a good sign (people remember your name).

## Is "Direct" bad?

Not on its own. A growing direct share can mean people remember your site. It's a problem only when it hides a source you care about, like a newsletter you'd want to measure.
