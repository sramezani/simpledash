A page that appears often in Google but gets few clicks for its position usually has a title or description that doesn't make people want to click. Find these pages in Search Console's Performance report, compare each one's click rate with your other pages at a similar position, and rewrite its title and description.

## Why compare with your own pages

Click rates depend heavily on position: the first result gets far more clicks than the eighth. So compare a page only with your own pages at about the same position, not with a general "good CTR" number you read somewhere.

## Find them

<Steps>
<Step>

Search Console → **Performance → Search results**, last 3 months, with all four numbers ticked.

</Step>
<Step>

Open the **Pages** tab and sort by **Impressions**.

</Step>
<Step>

Look at pages with many impressions. For pages with a similar **Average position**, compare their **CTR**. The ones well below their neighbours are your candidates.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Fix them

1. **Check what people searched.** Click the page, then **Queries**. Does your title match those searches?
2. **Rewrite the title.** Lead with what the searcher wants, in their words. Keep it under about 60 characters so it isn't cut off.
3. **Rewrite the description.** One or two sentences that answer the search and give a reason to click. Google may still choose its own snippet, but a good one is used often.
4. **Wait and compare.** Give it two to four weeks, then compare CTR before and after.

## When a low click rate is fine

- The search wants a quick answer that Google shows directly.
- The search isn't really about your page (your page ranks by accident).
- Big brands or ads take most clicks above you.
