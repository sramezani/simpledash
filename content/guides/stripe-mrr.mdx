MRR (monthly recurring revenue) is how much your active subscriptions bring in each month. Add up every active subscription's price as a monthly amount: a $20 monthly plan counts $20, and a $240 yearly plan also counts $20 ($240 ÷ 12). One-off payments don't count.

## How to calculate it

1. Take every active subscription.
2. Turn each price into a monthly amount: yearly ÷ 12, quarterly ÷ 3, weekly × 52 ÷ 12.
3. Multiply by quantity (for per-seat plans) and add them up.

**Example:** 40 customers on a $20 monthly plan and 10 on a $240 yearly plan:
40 × $20 + 10 × ($240 ÷ 12) = $800 + $200 = **$1,000 MRR**.

## What to include, and what not

| Include                                                                    | Leave out                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Active subscriptions                                                       | One-off payments and setup fees       |
| Subscriptions in a payment retry (past due), if you expect them to recover | Cancelled subscriptions               |
|                                                                            | Usage-based charges you can't predict |
|                                                                            | Taxes                                 |

Discounts are a choice: some count the list price, some the discounted price. Pick one and stay consistent.

## MRR vs revenue

Revenue is money that came in during a period, including one-off payments, minus refunds. MRR is a snapshot of what's recurring right now. A yearly plan brings in its full price on one day, but only 1/12 of it counts towards MRR.

## Where to see it in Stripe

Stripe's **Billing** overview shows MRR and subscription numbers for your account, calculated its own way. Small differences from your own calculation usually come from discounts, past-due subscriptions or currency conversion.
