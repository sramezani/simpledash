Google Analytics, Stripe, Search Console and your hosting provider's analytics each count something different, in a different way, so their numbers never match exactly. GA4 counts visitors who allowed it to run; Stripe counts every payment; Search Console counts clicks on Google; your host counts requests to its servers. Use each for what it measures best.

## What each one counts

| Tool                                                | Counts                                                                 | Misses                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Google Analytics 4**                              | Visits from browsers where its tag ran (and, with a banner, consented) | Ad-blocker users, declined consent, people who leave before the tag loads |
| **Stripe**                                          | Every successful payment and refund                                    | Nothing about where the buyer came from                                   |
| **Search Console**                                  | Clicks and impressions on Google search                                | Every other source; anonymized rare searches in per-search lists          |
| **Hosting analytics** (Vercel, Cloudflare, Netlify) | Requests or visits by their own method                                 | Often counts bots, or counts differently; no link to signups              |

## GA4 vs Stripe

- **Not every buyer is tracked.** A buyer who blocks GA4 still pays: Stripe counts them, GA4 doesn't.
- **Time zones.** Stripe's reports may use a different time zone from your GA4 property, so sales move between days.
- **Refunds and failed payments.** Stripe nets refunds; GA4 only knows what your tag sent.

Trust **Stripe for money**, and GA4 for where buyers came from.

## GA4 vs Search Console

- **Clicks aren't visits.** A Search Console click becomes a GA4 visit only if the tag runs and the visitor consents.
- **Different counting.** Search Console counts clicks by the page in Google's results; GA4 counts the page visitors landed on, which can differ after redirects.
- **Delay.** Search Console is usually 2 to 3 days behind.

Trust **Search Console for Google search**, and GA4 for what happened on your site after.

## GA4 vs your host's analytics

Hosting analytics often count every request, including bots, prefetches and visitors who block GA4. They can be higher or lower depending on their method. Compare trends, not totals.

## Which to trust?

Each is right about what it measures. Pick one source per question and compare it with itself over time: GA4 for visitors and sources, Stripe for revenue, Search Console for Google search.
