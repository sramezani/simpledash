To share Google Analytics 4 with someone, give their Google account the **Viewer** role in Admin → **Access management**: they can then see every report but change nothing. To share only some numbers, send a report as a PDF, or show them on a public stats page that needs no Google login.

## Which way to pick

| You want them to                          | Share this way                                     |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Look around your GA4 themselves           | [Give access](#give-someone-access)                |
| See one report, once or every week        | [Send a report](#send-a-report)                    |
| See a few numbers, without a Google login | [A public stats page](#show-your-numbers-publicly) |

## Give someone access

You need the **Administrator** role on the account or property.

<Steps>
<Step>

In GA4, open **Admin**. Under **Property** (one site) or **Account** (every site in it), click **Access management**.

</Step>
<Step>

Click **+**, then **Add users**.

</Step>
<Step>

Enter the email of their Google account and pick a role. **Viewer** is the safe choice: they can read reports and settings, and change nothing.

</Step>
<Step>

Tick **Notify new users by email** if you want GA4 to tell them, then click **Add**.

</Step>
</Steps>

To remove them later, go back to **Access management**, tick their name and click **Remove**.

### Which role to give

| Role          | Can                                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Viewer        | See reports and settings               |
| Analyst       | Also build explorations and share them |
| Marketer      | Also edit audiences and key events     |
| Editor        | Also change the property's settings    |
| Administrator | Everything, including who has access   |

Give the lowest role that does the job. An agency or a freelancer who changes your setup needs Editor; a co-founder or investor who only reads needs Viewer.

## Send a report

Open the report, click **Share this report** at the top right, then:

- **Download file** for a PDF or CSV you can attach to an email, or
- **Share link** to copy its address (it opens only for people who already have access), or
- **Schedule email** to send it every week ([how](/guides/google-analytics-weekly-email-report)).

## How do I share a report with someone who has no access?

Use **Download file** and send the PDF or CSV yourself. A shared link and a scheduled email only work for people who already have access to the property. For numbers that stay up to date without access, use a public page (next section).

## How should agencies and freelancers get access?

The site's owner keeps the GA4 account and property, and adds the agency's Google account (or a user group, for a team) with the role the work needs, at the property level. When the work ends, the owner removes it. That way the data never belongs to the agency, and nobody shares a password.

## Show your numbers publicly

GA4 has no public page. To show your visitors to anyone, such as followers when you build in public, an investor, or a sponsor, use a tool that publishes chosen numbers on a page without giving anyone your Google account. [How to share your Google Analytics stats publicly](/guides/public-analytics-page).

## Is it safe to share Google Analytics access?

Viewer access can't change your setup or delete data, but it shows everything in the property, including revenue if you track it. To hide money numbers, GA4's data restrictions (**No cost metrics**, **No revenue metrics**) can be set for each person in Access management.
