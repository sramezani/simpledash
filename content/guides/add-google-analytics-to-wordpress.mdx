On a self-hosted WordPress site, the easiest way is Google's official **Site Kit by Google** plugin: install it, sign in with Google and connect your GA4 property. On WordPress.com, paste your Measurement ID in the built-in setting (on plans that allow it).

## Option 1: Site Kit by Google (self-hosted)

<Steps>
<Step>

In WordPress admin, go to **Plugins → Add New**, search for **Site Kit by Google**, then **Install** and **Activate**.

</Step>
<Step>

Choose **Start setup** and sign in with the Google account that owns (or should own) your analytics.

</Step>
<Step>

When asked, connect **Analytics**: pick your existing GA4 property, or let Site Kit create one.

</Step>
<Step>

Finish the setup. Site Kit adds the Google tag to every page.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Option 2: paste the tag with a header plugin

If you'd rather not use Site Kit, install a plugin that adds code to your site's `<head>` (such as WPCode), and paste the Google tag from GA4 (Admin → Data streams → your stream → View tag instructions → Install manually). Don't use both options at once, or visits are counted twice.

Avoid pasting the tag into your theme's `header.php`: a theme update can remove it.

## Option 3: WordPress.com

On WordPress.com plans that allow it, paste your Measurement ID (`G-…`) into the Google Analytics setting in your site's settings, and save. WordPress.com's own [support page](https://wordpress.com/support/google-analytics/) shows exactly where it is on your plan.

## Check it works

Open your site in a private window and GA4's **Realtime overview**. If you're logged in to WordPress, some setups don't track admins: test in a private window. [More ways to check](/guides/check-ga4-is-working).

<InSimpleDash>
  Site Kit shows some numbers inside WordPress. SimpleDash shows the rest on one fast page, in plain
  words: sources, AI traffic, landing pages, live visitors, and signups once you pick your signup
  event. No plugin needed.
</InSimpleDash>
