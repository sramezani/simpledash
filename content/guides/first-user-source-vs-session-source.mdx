In Google Analytics 4, **first user source** is where a person came from the first time GA4 saw them, and it never changes. **Session source** is where each visit came from. A signup or a sale belongs to both: the channel that first found the person, and the visit it happened on. Comparing the two shows which channels find customers that other channels then get the credit for.

## The two in plain words

|                      | First user source                  | Session source                          |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| What it is           | Where the person came from first   | Where this visit came from              |
| Changes when?        | Never, for that person and browser | Every visit                             |
| Where GA4 shows it   | The User acquisition report        | The Traffic acquisition report          |
| A sale counts under… | The channel that found the person  | The channel of the visit they bought on |

Both skip direct visits when GA4 knows an earlier source: Google calls this "paid and organic channels last click". So someone who first came from X and later typed your address to buy still counts as X for that visit, not Direct.

## An example

Someone sees your post on Reddit and visits. A week later they search your name on Google, come back and buy.

- **First user source:** reddit.com
- **Session source** of the buying visit: google

In the Traffic acquisition report the sale sits under Google, and Reddit gets nothing. In User acquisition it sits under Reddit. Neither is wrong: Reddit found the customer, and Google brought them back.

## Compare them in GA4

<Steps>
<Step>

In GA4, open **Explore** and start a **Blank** exploration (Free form).

</Step>
<Step>

Under **Dimensions**, add **First user source** and **Session source**. Under **Metrics**, add **Event count**.

</Step>
<Step>

Drag both dimensions to **Rows** and Event count to **Values**.

</Step>
<Step>

Add a filter: **Event name** exactly matches your signup or purchase event, such as `sign_up` or `purchase`.

</Step>
</Steps>

Each row is now a path: first source, then the source of the visit with the event, and how many times it happened.

## What to look for

| What you see                                                 | What it tells you                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A source with many sales as first, few as session source     | It finds people who buy later, often through another channel           |
| Google or Direct with many sales from people found elsewhere | They bring people back; something else found them first                |
| A source with the same counts in both                        | People find you and buy in the same visit from it                      |
| Many "(not set)" first sources                               | Those people first came before GA4 was on your site, or GA4 lost track |

Keep to the counts. They show which channels found and brought back the people who signed up; they don't say why someone bought.

## Limits

- **Per browser.** GA4 knows a person by their browser (or your user ID, if you send one). Someone who switches device or clears cookies is a new person, with a new first source.
- **Events, not people.** Event count counts each signup or purchase, so one person who buys twice counts twice.
- **Not the attribution reports.** GA4's attribution reports, such as Model comparison, share each key event out between channels with a model, so they show parts like 1.3 sales. First user source and session source give whole counts.
