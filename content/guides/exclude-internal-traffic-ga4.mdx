To stop Google Analytics 4 counting your own visits, define your IP address as **internal traffic** in your data stream's tag settings, then turn GA4's **Internal Traffic** filter from Testing to **Active**. It matters most on small sites, where your own visits can be a big share of the total.

## 1. Find your IP address

Search "what is my IP" in Google, or ask your network admin for your office's address. Home connections often change their IP from time to time, so note when you set it.

## 2. Define internal traffic

<Steps>
<Step>

In GA4, open Admin → Data collection and modification → **Data streams**, and choose your web stream.

</Step>
<Step>

Under **Google tag**, choose **Configure tag settings**, then **Show more** and **Define internal traffic**.

</Step>
<Step>

Choose **Create**. Name the rule (for example "Home"), keep `traffic_type` as `internal`, and add **IP address equals** your IP. You can add several addresses or ranges.

</Step>
<Step>

**Create** to save.

</Step>
</Steps>

## 3. Turn the filter on

The rule only labels your visits. To exclude them:

1. Admin → Data collection and modification → **Data filters**.
2. Open **Internal Traffic** and change its state from **Testing** to **Active**, then save.

<ImageSlot
  id="ga4-internal-traffic-filter"
  alt="GA4's Data filters with the Internal Traffic filter set to Active"
>
  In GA4, Admin → Data filters → Internal Traffic. Capture the filter's settings with the "Active"
  filter state selected.
</ImageSlot>

Once active, the filter is permanent for new data: excluded visits can't be brought back. Testing first lets you check it matches the right visits.

## Check it works

While the filter is in **Testing**, open Reports → Realtime and add a comparison on **Test data filter name** to see your own visits labelled. Once active, your visits stop appearing in Realtime.

## Changing IP addresses

If your IP changes often, also exclude yourself with a browser extension that blocks Google Analytics on your own site, or keep the filter's rule updated. Don't rely on just one method.
