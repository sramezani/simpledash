A public stats page shows your site's numbers to anyone with the link: visitors, a chart, top pages, where people come from. Founders who build in public use one to share progress honestly. Share traffic and growth, keep anything that identifies customers private, and pick a tool that shows only what you choose.

## Why share your numbers

- **Trust.** Real numbers are more convincing than "we're growing fast".
- **Accountability.** A public page keeps you honest with yourself.
- **Content.** Launches, spikes and milestones become posts people want to read.
- **Feedback.** People point out things you missed: a page that works, a source you ignored.

## What to share, and what to keep private

| Usually fine to share   | Keep private                        |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Visitors and page views | Anything about individual visitors  |
| The visitors chart      | Customer names or emails            |
| Top pages and sources   | Your notes and internal plans       |
| Countries and devices   | Exact revenue, unless you choose to |
| Live visitors           | Your analytics account details      |

## Can Google Analytics make a public page?

Not directly: GA4 reports need a Google sign-in with access to the property. You can build a shared report in Looker Studio, but it takes setup, and you need to check carefully what it exposes.

## Set one up with SimpleDash (free)

<Steps>
<Step>

[Sign in to SimpleDash](/signin) with the Google account that has your GA4 property.

</Step>
<Step>

On the dashboard, choose **Turn on** in "Share your stats publicly?", or open the site's settings and turn on **Public page**.

</Step>
<Step>

Choose the cards to show. Visitors, the chart, sources, pages and countries are on by default; add AI traffic, devices or live visitors if you like.

</Step>
<Step>

Copy the link (`simpledash.app/p/your-site`) and share it.

</Step>
</Steps>

Chart notes, your email address and your GA4 property ID never appear on it. [See a live example](/demo).

## Tips for building in public

- **Share the link once, then post milestones.** "1,000 visitors this month" with the page link beats a screenshot every day.
- **Explain the spikes.** A sentence like "launched on Product Hunt" makes the chart a story.
- **Be consistent.** Same day each week or month, good weeks and bad.
