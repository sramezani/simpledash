An **engaged session** is a visit that lasted longer than 10 seconds, had a key event, or viewed at least two pages. **Engagement rate** is the share of sessions that were engaged, and GA4's **bounce rate** is simply the opposite: the share that weren't.

## The rule for an engaged session

A session is engaged if **any** of these is true:

- it lasted **longer than 10 seconds** (with the page in view), or
- it had a **key event**, or
- it had **two or more page or screen views**.

You can raise the 10 seconds (up to 60) in your data stream's tag settings (Adjust timer for engaged sessions).

## Engagement rate and bounce rate

- **Engagement rate** = engaged sessions ÷ all sessions.
- **Bounce rate** = 100% − engagement rate.

So if 60% of sessions were engaged, the bounce rate is 40%.

## Why GA4's bounce rate isn't the old one

In Universal Analytics, a bounce was a single-page visit, even if someone read for five minutes. In GA4, that same visit counts as engaged (over 10 seconds), so it's not a bounce. GA4 bounce rates are usually lower, and more meaningful, than the old ones.

## Is my bounce rate good?

Compare it with itself over time and between your own pages, not with numbers from other sites. A blog post that answers a question in one page can have a high bounce rate and still do its job.

## In SimpleDash

SimpleDash shows **Avg. time** rather than bounce rate, because "how long people stayed with the page in view" is easier to read. [Average engagement time](/glossary/engagement-time).
