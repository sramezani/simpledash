A **session** in Google Analytics 4 is one visit: a period when someone is using your site. It starts when they arrive and ends after 30 minutes without activity (by default). One person can have many sessions, so you'll always have more sessions than users.

## When a session starts and ends

- **Starts:** when someone opens your site and GA4 records `session_start`.
- **Ends:** after 30 minutes of no activity. You can change this timeout in the data stream's tag settings (Adjust session timeout).

Unlike the old Universal Analytics, GA4 **doesn't** start a new session at midnight or when the traffic source changes mid-visit.

## Sessions vs users vs views

|                          | Counts                                | In plain words |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| **Users** (active users) | People (really, browsers and devices) | Visitors       |
| **Sessions**             | Visits: one person can make many      | Visits         |
| **Views**                | Pages opened: one visit can open many | Page views     |

If 100 people visit twice each and read 3 pages each time, that's 100 users, 200 sessions and 600 views. So there are always at least as many sessions as users, and at least as many views as sessions.

"4 sessions" means four separate visits, from one person or several.

## Which number should I use?

- **Users** for "how many people came". It's the clearest growth number, and the one GA4 shows first.
- **Sessions** for "where did visits come from", because each visit has its own source.
- **Views** for "which pages are read most".

Sessions and views grow when the same people come back or read more, so they can rise while the number of people stays flat.

## Why new and returning users don't add up

New users plus returning users can be more than total users: someone who visited for the first time and came back in the same period counts once in each. [Active users vs total and new users](/glossary/active-users).

## Session source

Each session has one source and medium: where that visit came from. Traffic acquisition reports use session-level sources. [Source and medium](/glossary/source-medium).

## In SimpleDash

SimpleDash leads with **Visitors** (active users) rather than sessions, because "how many people came" is the clearer question. Sources are by session, like GA4's Traffic acquisition report.
