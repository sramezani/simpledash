Indie hackers usually want three things from analytics: how many people came, where from, and did anyone sign up or pay. You can get that by keeping Google Analytics 4 and reading it through a simpler dashboard, or by switching to a lightweight tracker with its own script. Here are the honest options.

## First decision: keep GA4 or replace it?

| Keep GA4 if                       | Replace it if                                               |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| You already have it, with history | You're starting fresh and want no Google                    |
| You want it free                  | You'd pay for privacy and simplicity                        |
| You may need its depth later      | You'd rather avoid a cookie banner (check your local rules) |

## Keep GA4

- **SimpleDash**: GA4 on one page in plain words, AI traffic, live visitors, a public stats page, and a Monday email, Search Console and Stripe revenue on Pro. Nothing added to your site. [Compare with GA4](/compare/simpledash-vs-google-analytics).
- **Looker Studio**: build your own GA4 dashboard. [Compare](/compare/simpledash-vs-looker-studio).

## Replace GA4

- **Plausible**: open source, cookie-free, EU-hosted. [Compare](/compare/simpledash-vs-plausible).
- **Fathom**: privacy-first, polished, cookie-free. [Compare](/compare/simpledash-vs-fathom).
- **Umami**: open source, self-host or cloud. [Compare](/compare/simpledash-vs-umami).
- **DataFast**: founder-focused, with revenue attribution. [Compare](/compare/simpledash-vs-datafast).
- **Vercel Web Analytics**: built into Vercel. [Compare](/compare/simpledash-vs-vercel-analytics).

## What matters most for indie hackers

1. **Signups, not just visits.** Make sure whichever tool you pick counts signups. In GA4: [track signups](/guides/track-signups-ga4).
2. **Source of launches.** Tag your launch links with [UTM links](/guides/utm-links).
3. **Low maintenance.** You have a product to build; pick something you'll actually open every week.
4. **Sharing.** If you build in public, a [public stats page](/guides/public-analytics-page) is worth having.
